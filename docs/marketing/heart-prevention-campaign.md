**Article 2: Understanding Arrhythmias**
```
Arrhythmias Explained: What Your Heart Is Telling You

As an engineer who built an ECG monitoring system, I've spent countless hours studying heart rhythms. Here's what I wish more people understood.

What is an Arrhythmia?
Simply put: an irregular heartbeat. Your heart's electrical system misfires, causing it to beat too fast, too slow, or irregularly.

Types (and what they mean):

1. Atrial Fibrillation (AFib)
   • Most common serious arrhythmia
   • Affects 6.1 million Americans (likely underreported)
   • Increases stroke risk 5x
   • Often no symptoms
   • Detection: Irregular R-R intervals on ECG

2. Premature Ventricular Contractions (PVCs)
   • Extra heartbeats originating from ventricles
   • Very common, usually harmless
   • Can feel like "heart skip" or "flutter"
   • Concern if frequent (>10% of beats)

3. Bradycardia
   • Heart rate <60 BPM
   • Can be normal (athletes) or problematic
   • Symptoms: fatigue, dizziness, fainting

4. Tachycardia
   • Heart rate >100 BPM at rest
   • Can indicate stress, dehydration, or arrhythmia
   • Sustained episodes need evaluation

Why Detection Matters:
• 1 in 4 people with AFib don't know they have it
• Silent AFib is just as dangerous as symptomatic
• Early treatment reduces complications
• Some arrhythmias worsen over time

The Tech Approach:
Traditional: Spot checks at doctor's office (miss 80% of episodes)
Holter monitor: 24-48 hour rental ($$$)
Consumer: Fitbit/Apple Watch (basic detection)
Continuous: DIY ECG monitoring (what I built)

My Learning:
Building this system taught me that heart health isn't just about beating—it's about rhythm, variability, and patterns. The data tells stories that symptoms alone can't.

Real example from my monitoring:
During exercise: HR jumped to 175 BPM (expected)
But AI caught: R-R intervals showed pattern suggesting early PVCs
Action: Reduced caffeine, improved hydration
Result: Episodes decreased by 70%

This is preventive care in action.

If you have family history of heart disease, AFib, or stroke—consider monitoring beyond annual checkups.

Your heart is talking. Are you listening?

#Cardiology #HealthTech #ArrhythmiaAwareness #AFib #PreventiveMedicine

[Infographic showing different arrhythmia patterns]
```

🫀 Let's talk about Heart Rate Variability (HRV)

Most people track heart rate. But HRV might be more important.

Here's what HRV tells you (and why you should care):

🧵 (1/8)

---

HRV measures the variation in time between each heartbeat.

High HRV = good (heart adapts well to stress)
Low HRV = potential issue (heart stuck in stress mode)

It's a window into your nervous system health.

(2/8)

---

Example:
Your resting heart rate is 60 BPM.

But beats aren't exactly 1 second apart:
• Beat 1-2: 0.95 seconds
• Beat 2-3: 1.05 seconds
• Beat 3-4: 0.98 seconds

This variation = HRV

More variation = healthier

(3/8)

---

Why does HRV matter?

Low HRV associated with:
• Stress and anxiety
• Poor sleep quality
• Overtraining
• Cardiovascular issues
• Inflammation

It's an early warning system.

(4/8)

---

What affects HRV?

⬆️ Increases HRV:
• Good sleep
• Meditation
• Exercise (moderate)
• Healthy diet
• Recovery time

⬇️ Decreases HRV:
• Stress
• Poor sleep
• Alcohol
• Overtraining
• Illness

(5/8)

---

How to measure HRV:

Most fitness trackers measure it:
• Apple Watch
• Fitbit
• Garmin
• Oura Ring
• Chest strap monitors

Best time: First thing in the morning, before getting up.

Track trends, not single readings.

(6/8)

---

What's a good HRV?

There's no universal number. It varies by:
• Age (decreases with age)
• Fitness level
• Genetics
• Individual baseline

Focus on YOUR trend:
Increasing = good
Decreasing = investigate why

(7/8)

---

Bottom line:

Heart rate tells you the speed.
HRV tells you the flexibility.

Both matter for complete heart health.

Track it. Understand it. Use it to optimize your health.

Your heart is adaptable. Let's keep it that way.

(8/8)

#HRV #HeartHealth #WellnessTips
